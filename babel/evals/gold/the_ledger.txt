Chapter 7: The Ledger

The counting house was cold. Captain Ilse Varga stood by the window while Brother Aldric paged through the ledger, and Pell, the clerk, hovered near the door with his hands knotted together.

"Three hundred crowns," Ilse said. "Gone in a week."

Aldric did not look up. "The ledger balances."

"Ledgers can be made to balance."

"Are you accusing me, Captain?"

"I'm asking where the money went."

She turned from the window. Pell flinched as if she had raised a hand to him.

"Pell," she said, "you kept the night book. Who signed for the shipment on the ninth?"

"I— it was— the brother did, ma'am. Brother Aldric signed."

"For the church's share," Aldric said quickly. "That was agreed. You were there, Pell. Tell her."

"I was there."

"And?"

"And he signed for the church's share." Pell swallowed. "And then he signed again."

The room went very still.

Aldric closed the ledger. *The boy counts better than I thought.*

"A clerical error," he murmured. "Easily mended."

"Then mend it," said Ilse. "By morning. Every crown.

"And Pell stays with me until it's done."

* * *

Outside, the bells began to ring for vespers.
